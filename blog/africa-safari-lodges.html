The Top 5 Luxury Safari Lodges in Africa (2026)

By The Affluent Traveler · 8 min read · Updated November 2026

There is a particular silence at dawn on the savanna, broken only by a lion’s distant call and the clink of a silver coffee pot brought to your suite.

The modern luxury safari has moved far beyond canvas and campfires. Today’s finest lodges offer private vehicles, expert guides and trackers, spa pavilions, cellar-led dining and suites with their own plunge pools. After weighing wildlife, service, design and sense of place, these are the five lodges we would book in 2026.

01Singita Sasakwa Lodge

Country TanzaniaRegion Serengeti, Grumeti ReserveFrom approx. $3,000 per night

Perched on a hill above the Grumeti plains, Sasakwa feels like an Edwardian country estate transplanted to the Serengeti. Cottages are generously sized, with private terraces, plunge pools and sweeping views, while the main house offers a spa, tennis courts and a wine cellar that rivals many European restaurants.

The reserve is private, which means off-road driving and night drives are possible, and your vehicle is rarely shared. Between June and September the Grumeti River crossings draw some of the continent’s most dramatic wildlife encounters. Expect rates from roughly $3,000 per night for two guests, fully inclusive.

02Mombo Camp

Country BotswanaRegion Okavango DeltaFrom approx. $2,800 per night

Set on Chief’s Island, Mombo is widely considered one of the finest wildlife-viewing locations in Africa. Lion, leopard and wild dog are frequent sightings, and the camp’s raised, canvas-and-timber suites combine sweeping views with a refined, understated finish. Each has a private deck and plunge pool.

Game drives are led by exceptionally skilled guides, and exclusive access to the surrounding concession means fewer vehicles at every sighting. Peak-season rates, from July to October, typically range from $2,800 to $4,500 per night for two guests, all inclusive.

03Royal Malewane

Country South AfricaRegion Greater KrugerFrom approx. $2,500 per night

For pure indulgence, Royal Malewane is hard to match. Colonial-inspired suites come with fireplaces, deep baths and private plunge pools, and the Royal Suites and Africa House add a dedicated vehicle, ranger, tracker and chef for the ultimate in privacy. Its Africology Spa is among the finest in the bush.

Private game drives are the hallmark here, with guides and trackers who bring deep local knowledge, plus walking safaris and a celebrated dining experience. Standard suites start around $2,500 per night for two, while private villas can exceed $10,000.

04Angama Mara

Country KenyaRegion Maasai MaraFrom approx. $2,000 per night

Angama Mara sits on the edge of the Great Rift Valley escarpment, looking over the Mara Triangle, with views that seem to stretch to the end of the world. Suites are generous, light-filled and finished with a contemporary eye, and every one has a private deck made for watching the plains.

Between July and October the Great Migration brings thousands of wildebeest to the Mara River. Guests can also enjoy a hot-air balloon flight at sunrise, followed by a bush breakfast. Expect rates of roughly $2,000 to $3,500 per night for two guests, fully inclusive.

05&Beyond Sossusvlei Desert Lodge

Country NamibiaRegion NamibRand Nature ReserveFrom approx. $2,000 per night

A different kind of safari, and a memorable one. Set within a private reserve beside the Namib’s towering red dunes, this lodge offers sleek, glass-fronted suites with their own telescopes, and some even allow you to sleep beneath the stars on a private skybed.

Days are spent exploring the dunes and dramatic desert landscapes, with scenic flights, guided nature drives and some of the clearest night skies on Earth. Rates typically begin around $2,000 per night for two guests, making it an elegant and distinctive addition to a longer southern African journey.

Planning Your Safari

The dry season, from roughly June to October, offers the best wildlife viewing across East and Southern Africa, as animals gather around water and the vegetation thins. It is also the most expensive and busiest time, so the finest lodges are often booked a year ahead. The shoulder months bring lower rates, lush landscapes and far fewer vehicles.

Many of these lodges are reached by private charter flights or small bush planes, so plan transfers carefully and pack in soft-sided bags. A malaria prophylaxis consultation and the right travel insurance, including medical evacuation cover, are sensible steps well before departure. Combining two or three lodges in one journey, such as a Botswana delta camp followed by a Cape Town stay, is the most rewarding way to see the continent.

Your Safari Awaits

The most sought-after suites sell out a year in advance, especially during the migration. Secure your dates before they are gone.
